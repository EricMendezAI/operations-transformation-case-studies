# Constraint-to-Outcome Method

A simple framework for moving from an ambiguous operating problem to a measurable result.

## 1. Establish the Current State

Before proposing a solution:
- understand the workflow as it actually operates,
- identify who owns each part,
- establish the baseline,
- separate symptoms from the underlying constraint.

## 2. Define the Constraint

Ask:
- What is limiting throughput, growth, quality, or customer experience?
- Where does work wait?
- Where does rework occur?
- What decisions are unclear?
- What behavior is the current system encouraging?

## 3. Design the Intervention

The answer may involve:
- process redesign,
- clearer accountability,
- training,
- tooling,
- automation,
- reporting,
- staffing,
- quality standards,
- or changes to how teams work together.

The objective is not to create a more complicated process. It is to remove the constraint with the least unnecessary complexity.

## 4. Align the People Doing the Work

Operational changes fail when the people closest to the workflow do not understand:
- why it is changing,
- what they own,
- what good looks like,
- how performance will be measured.

## 5. Drive Through Implementation

A recommendation is not an outcome.

Stay close enough to:
- identify breakdowns,
- remove friction,
- reinforce new behaviors,
- adjust what does not work,
- prevent the organization from reverting to the old process.

## 6. Measure Before and After

Use the metrics tied to the original constraint.

Examples:
- cycle time
- throughput
- revenue
- conversion
- churn
- rework
- delivery time
- customer experience
- adoption

## 7. Make the Change Durable

The work is not finished until:
- ownership is clear,
- the process can run without constant intervention,
- metrics are visible,
- the improvement can survive leadership attention moving elsewhere.
