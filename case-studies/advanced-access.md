# Advanced Access — Churn Reduction and Sales Quality

## Context

At Advanced Access, I progressed from sales representative to Sales Manager, QA Manager, and ultimately Director of Sales. In leadership, I was responsible for eight sales managers and more than 40 commission-based representatives.

One of the most important operating problems was customer churn.

## Operating Constraint

Monthly churn was approximately **45%**.

A churn problem at that level cannot be treated only as a retention issue after the sale. It signals that something upstream in the operating system may be creating customers who are poorly qualified, poorly set up, poorly served, or entering with expectations that cannot be sustained.

## Approach

The response required changes across the sales and customer experience system, including:
- stronger training,
- clearer accountability,
- quality management,
- closer attention to how representatives sold and set expectations,
- improved coaching,
- stronger performance standards.

Because I had experience in both sales leadership and quality management, I was able to look at the issue from both sides: how revenue was being created and what happened to the customer after the sale.

## Measurable Outcome

Monthly customer churn was reduced from approximately **45% to below 20%**.

## Why It Matters

The key lesson was that revenue quality matters as much as revenue volume.

A sales organization can appear productive while creating downstream problems that eventually destroy the value it generated. Solving churn required treating acquisition, quality, expectations, coaching, and retention as parts of the same system.

## Transferable Operating Lesson

When a downstream metric is failing, do not assume the downstream team owns the root cause.

Trace the problem backward through:
1. how the customer entered,
2. what was promised,
3. how the work was handed off,
4. how performance was monitored,
5. where accountability broke down.

That systems view is often where the real constraint becomes visible.
